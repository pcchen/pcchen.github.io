# Real-Space RG for the 2D Ising Model on a Square Lattice with Decimation

Consider the ferromagnetic Ising model on a two-dimensional square lattice with nearest-neighbor interactions,

$$
H=-J\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\sigma_i\sigma_j,
\qquad \sigma_i=\pm1,\quad J>0.
$$

The partition function is

$$
Z_N(K)=\sum_{\{\sigma\}}\exp\left(K\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\sigma_i\sigma_j\right),
$$

where $K=J/(k_BT)$. Construct a real-space renormalization group (RG) transformation using the decimation method and estimate the critical temperature and critical exponents.

## (a) Decimation of the square lattice

Divide the lattice into two sublattices, $A$ and $B$, according to a checkerboard pattern. Trace out all spins belonging to sublattice $B$.

```text
●───○───●───○───●
│   │   │   │   │
○───●───○───●───○
│   │   │   │   │
●───○───●───○───●
│   │   │   │   │
○───●───○───●───○
│   │   │   │   │
●───○───●───○───●

● : retained spin (A)
○ : eliminated spin (B)
```

The retained sites form the following lattice (only its nearest-neighbor bonds are shown):

```text
●       ●       ●
  ╲   ╱   ╲   ╱
    ●       ●
  ╱   ╲   ╱   ╲
●       ●       ●
  ╲   ╱   ╲   ╱
    ●       ●
  ╱   ╲   ╱   ╲
●       ●       ●
```

1. Show that the remaining spins form a square lattice rotated by $45^\circ$ relative to the original lattice.
2. Determine the length rescaling factor $b=a'/a$ and the number of remaining spins $N'$.
3. Show that the partition function can be expressed as

   $$
   Z_N(K)=\sum_{\{\sigma_A\}}\prod_{i\in B}2\cosh\left(K\sum_{j\in\mathrm{nn}(i)}\sigma_j\right),
   $$

   where $\mathrm{nn}(i)$ denotes the four nearest neighbors of site $i$.

## (b) Effective interactions

Consider a spin $\sigma_0$ on sublattice $B$ and its four neighboring spins, labeled cyclically.

```text
       σ₁                       σ₁
       │                       ╱│╲
       │                      ╱ │ ╲
σ₄ ─── σ₀ ─── σ₂    ──→    σ₄ ─── σ₂
       │                      ╲ │ ╱
       │                       ╲│╱
       σ₃                       σ₃

 Before decimation       Six pairwise bonds
                         + four-spin interaction
```

After tracing out $\sigma_0$, the local Boltzmann weight becomes

$$
W(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3,\sigma_4)
=2\cosh[K(\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3+\sigma_4)].
$$

Show that this weight can be written exactly as

$$
W=A(K)\exp\left[B(K)\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq4}\sigma_i\sigma_j+C(K)\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_4\right].
$$

By considering the following three representative spin configurations, determine $A(K)$, $B(K)$, and $C(K)$ analytically.

| Configuration | $\sum_i\sigma_i$ | $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i\sigma_j$ | $\prod_i\sigma_i$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $(+,+,+,+)$ | | | |
| $(+,+,+,-)$ | | | |
| $(+,+,-,-)$ | | | |

Explain why these three configurations are sufficient to determine all three coefficients.

## (c) Renormalized Hamiltonian

Combining the contributions from all eliminated spins, show that the renormalized Hamiltonian takes the form

$$
-\beta H'=G'+K_1'\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle'}\sigma_i\sigma_j
+K_2'\sum_{\langle\!\langle i,j\rangle\!\rangle'}\sigma_i\sigma_j
+K_4'\sum_{\square'}\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_4,
$$

where the three sums represent nearest-neighbor interactions, next-nearest-neighbor interactions, and four-spin plaquette interactions generated by decimation, respectively.

Determine $G'$, $K_1'$, $K_2'$, and $K_4'$ in terms of $A(K)$, $B(K)$, $C(K)$, and $N$. Pay particular attention to the number of eliminated spins contributing to each effective bond. Explain why the original nearest-neighbor Ising Hamiltonian is not closed under the exact RG transformation.

## (d) Approximate RG transformation

To obtain a closed RG recursion relation involving a single coupling constant, adopt the approximation

$$
K'=K_1'+K_2',
$$

and neglect the four-spin interaction $K_4'$.

Show that the resulting recursion relation is

$$
\boxed{K'=\frac{3}{8}\ln\cosh(4K).}
$$

Discuss why this recursion relation is approximate, even though the decimation in part (b) is exact.

## (e) Fixed points and RG flows

Determine the fixed points of the RG transformation

$$
K^*=R(K^*),\qquad R(K)=\frac{3}{8}\ln\cosh(4K).
$$

1. Identify the high-temperature and low-temperature fixed points.
2. Find the nontrivial fixed point $K^*$ numerically.
3. Determine the stability of each fixed point.
4. Sketch the RG flow diagram.
5. Estimate the critical temperature $k_BT_c/J$ and compare it with the exact result $K_c=\frac12\ln(1+\sqrt{2})$.

## (f) Critical exponent of the correlation length

Define the deviation from the nontrivial fixed point as $g=K-K^*$. Linearize the RG transformation near $K^*$ to obtain

$$
g'=\lambda_t g+O(g^2).
$$

1. Calculate the thermal RG eigenvalue $\lambda_t$.
2. Determine the thermal scaling exponent $y_t$ from $\lambda_t=b^{y_t}$.
3. Using the RG transformation of the correlation length, $\xi(g)=b\,\xi(g')$, derive $\nu=1/y_t$.
4. Estimate $\nu$ numerically and compare it with the exact Ising exponent $\nu=1$.

## (g) Free-energy scaling and specific heat

Define the dimensionless free energy per site,

$$
\phi(K)=-\frac{1}{N}\ln Z_N(K).
$$

1. Using the constant term generated during decimation, derive the recursion relation for $\phi(K)$ under the approximate RG transformation.
2. Show that the singular part of the free energy satisfies

   $$
   \phi_s(g)=b^{-2}\phi_s(b^{y_t}g).
   $$

3. Derive the hyperscaling relation $\alpha=2-2\nu$.
4. Evaluate $\alpha$ using your RG estimate of $\nu$ and compare it with the exact result $\alpha=0$, corresponding to a logarithmically divergent specific heat.

## (h) Discussion

- What happens if one retains only $K_1'$ instead of using $K'=K_1'+K_2'$? Does the resulting RG transformation still possess a nontrivial finite-temperature fixed point?
- Why does iterating the exact RG transformation generate increasingly complicated interactions?
- How might one systematically improve the approximation?
